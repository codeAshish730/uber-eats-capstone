UBER EATS CAPSTONE - DAY 3
SENTIMENT ANALYSIS EVALUATION

1. DATASET
- Source: data/raw/reviews.csv
- Total reviews: 500
- Text was lowercased, tokenized, stripped of punctuation,
  filtered for stop words, and lemmatized.
- Cleaned data: data/processed/cleaned_reviews.csv

2. TF-IDF
- TF-IDF matrix: data/processed/reviews_tfidf.npz
- Feature list: data/processed/tfidf_features.csv
- Matrix dimensions: 500 reviews x 32 features
- Unigrams and bigrams were included.

3. SUPERVISED SENTIMENT MODEL
- Algorithm: Linear Support Vector Classifier (LinearSVC)
- Labels: ratings 1-2 negative, 3 neutral, 4-5 positive
- Test split: 20%, stratified
- Accuracy: 0.37
- Macro F1-score: 0.33
- Model: models/review_sentiment_model.joblib

4. VADER
- Accuracy: 0.386
- Macro F1-score: 0.32
- Negative F1-score: 0.41
- Neutral F1-score: 0.08
- Positive F1-score: 0.48
- Predictions: data/processed/vader_review_predictions.csv

5. KEY FINDINGS
- VADER had 38.6% accuracy on all 500 reviews.
- LinearSVC had 37% accuracy on its 100-review test set.
- These scores should not be compared as a controlled benchmark
  because the evaluation sets differ.
- Neutral sentiment was difficult for both approaches.
- Frequent terms included food, order, delivery, cold, and late.
- Repeated synthetic review text had inconsistent ratings,
  making rating-derived sentiment labels noisy.

6. LIMITATIONS
- Review text is synthetic and highly repetitive.
- Identical text sometimes has different ratings.
- Ratings are weak sentiment labels, not verified ground truth.
- Results may not generalize to real customer reviews.
- Prep time was not available in the source data.

7. BUSINESS IMPLICATIONS
- Food temperature and delivery delays are candidate complaint
  themes worth investigating in real customer feedback.
- Collect more varied, realistic reviews and validate sentiment
  labels before deploying an automated sentiment system.
- Review delivery performance and food quality alongside
  customer ratings before making operational decisions.
